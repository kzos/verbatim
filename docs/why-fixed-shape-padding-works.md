# Why fixed-shape padding buys batch invariance, and what it costs

**Measured on an NVIDIA B300 SXM6, NeMo 3.1.0+abb8254da, torch 2.11.0+cu128, bfloat16, 160 ms chunks,
256 LibriSpeech test-other utterances, 2026-09-13 and 2026-09-14.** Every number below comes from a
record under `rows/exploratory/`. The figure is `figures/padding-vs-ragged-b300.png`, regenerated by
`probes/padding_vs_ragged_figure.py`.

## The claim

A session's transcript is a function of that session's audio alone. It does not depend on how many
other sessions share the GPU, or on which ones, or on when they joined and left.

That is not how batched GPU inference usually behaves, and it is the whole reason this server exists.

## Why concurrency changes a transcript at all

Floating-point addition is not associative: `(a + b) + c` and `a + (b + c)` can differ in the last
bits. GPU kernels exploit that freedom. A matmul or a reduction is split across threads and tiles, and
**the split is chosen from the shape of the tensor** — including its batch dimension. Change the batch
size and the library may pick a different tile size, a different split-k, a different reduction tree.
The arithmetic is equally correct; the rounding is not identical.

Those last bits reach the transcript because a streaming ASR decoder makes discrete choices. Most of
the time a token's margin over its runner-up is comfortable and a rounding difference changes nothing.
Occasionally the margin is thin, the argmax flips, and one word becomes another — or a word boundary
moves by one frame. At bfloat16, with roughly eight bits of mantissa, thin margins are common enough
that about a quarter of utterances flip somewhere.

This is not a NeMo defect and not a bug. Trading exactness for speed is what reduced precision is for.

## What the design does about it

`scheduler/buckets.py` pads the steady batch to exactly `B` rows at every occupancy, using persistent
pad rows that hold reserved negative stream ids. With twelve live sessions and `B = 128`, the encoder
is handed 128 rows: twelve real and 116 pads.

So the batch dimension **never changes**. The kernel library sees one shape for the life of the
process, picks one tiling, and every row's arithmetic is reproducible regardless of who else is
connected. The property is bought by removing the variable, not by making the arithmetic exact.

Two details matter and are easy to get wrong:

- **Pad rows carry samples, so they cannot be told apart by counting valid rows.** They are identified
  by their negative stream id. An earlier version of the invariance gate counted rows and was blind to
  a deliberately injected leak until that was fixed.
- **Final frames are peeled out of the steady batch** into a separate eager side-batch, because
  upstream never captures a `keep_all_outputs=True` call. The steady batch therefore never contains an
  `is_last` frame, which is what keeps its shape constant even as sessions end.

## The measurement, and the control that makes it one

A passing invariance test on a padded server proves very little on its own: the padding is what the
test is checking, so passing is close to a restatement of the design. The question a reader is
entitled to ask is *what outcome would have made this fail?*

So `--padding ragged` was built: the steady batch becomes exactly the live rows and the edge batches
exactly the finals, with no pad row anywhere. Everything else is identical. Both arms run eager, so the
CUDA-graph path is not a variable.

| arm | verdict | streams differing from concurrency 1 |
|---|---|---|
| fixed, constant occupancy | **invariant** | 0 of 256 |
| fixed, occupancy churned 1 → 42 → 1 every 20 s | **invariant** | 0 of 256 |
| ragged, constant occupancy | divergent | 66 of 256 |
| ragged, occupancy churned | divergent | **88 of 256** |

Three things in that table are worth more than the headline.

**The two runs at concurrency 32 agree in every arm.** The ragged server is perfectly deterministic
*at a given concurrency* and non-deterministic *across* concurrencies. That is batch dependence, not
run-to-run noise, and it is exactly the property the fixed arm removes.

**Churn makes ragged worse, as predicted in advance.** 66 → 88. When the shape moves *within* a
session's life rather than merely differing between runs, more transcripts break. The prediction was
written into `docs/decisions/0014` before the run.

**Churn does not touch the fixed arm.** A session whose neighbour count swings from 1 to 42 and back,
repeatedly, produces a byte-identical transcript to the same audio run alone.

Divergence appears in both channels the gate compares. One stream reads *"they"* at concurrency 1 and
*"there"* at 42; word timings move by a frame — for instance `away` at 1280–1440 ms becoming
1200–1440 ms.

## Consistency with the earlier precision findings

`docs/batch-invariance-findings.md` reports 287 of 2,939 utterances diverging at bfloat16 on a
different probe. That probe varied the **batch size** — one utterance alone against the same utterance
in a batch of N — which is the variable this server pins. Its own control, *"batch shape pinned,
contents varied"*, returned zero differences in 1,024 comparisons.

The two results agree completely, and between them they isolate the mechanism: **the divergence
follows the shape, not the contents.**

## The price — WITHDRAWN 2026-09-14, and why

This section previously read: *"Batch invariance costs 28.8% of sustained capacity on this die"*, from
a matched pair of capacity searches — ragged 59 sustained streams, fixed 42 — differing only in the
padding.

**That comparison does not support that number, and the figure is withdrawn.** Each arm was a single
seed at shortened durations. A later search on the same server at frozen durations over three seeds
(`rows/exploratory/capacity-canonical-b300-2026-09-14.json`) gives:

| seed | sustained streams |
|---|---|
| 20260914 | 20 |
| 20260915 | 24 |
| 20260916 | **46** |

**The spread on one unchanged configuration is 26 streams, a factor of 2.3.** The fixed-versus-ragged
difference was 17 streams from one seed each. The noise is larger than the effect, so the paired
observation establishes a direction at best and a price not at all.

What survives: the two arms *were* matched on everything but the padding, and ragged came out higher.
That is consistent with padding costing capacity, which it must — it spends compute on rows that exist
only to hold a shape. How much is unmeasured, and a three-seed paired comparison is what would measure
it.

What still holds independently of any of this: padding costs **compute, not memory**. The NeMo slot
table is allocated up front for the full bucket either way, so both arms run 271 slots.

The industry anchor is left here because it is someone else's measurement and is unaffected:
batch-invariant kernel work in LLM serving — fixing the reductions rather than pinning the shape — is
published at roughly 34–62% throughput cost. Whether this server's approach is cheaper than that is now
an open question rather than a claim.

## What actually ends a capacity run

Neither arm ended on latency. Both ended `unstable`, with the last passing rung at 207–217 ms against
a 310 ms budget — nowhere near it.

The warm-up p95 reading spread is monotone in load: the fixed arm stays under 6% to 41 streams, then
39.4% at 42 and 62.2% at 43; the ragged arm stays under 10% to 59 and crosses at 60. **What grows with
concurrency first is latency variance, not latency.** The convergence detector refuses to open a
measurement window on a load that has not settled, so the reported figure is *the highest concurrency
at which the server settles* — stricter than the highest it can serve, and worth saying plainly.

## What is not claimed

- **No capacity price is claimed.** See the withdrawal above. The capacity numbers here are
  exploratory, and the seed-to-seed spread on one configuration is larger than any difference between
  arms yet measured.
- **This is bfloat16 only.** float32 was not run. The property is held by the padding, and at bfloat16
  the padding is the *only* thing holding it: if a shape ever did change, bfloat16 diverges far more
  than float32 would (287 against 6 on the earlier probe). float32 would be a second line of defence,
  and there is not one today.
- **One die, one checkpoint, one chunk mode, one corpus of 256 utterances.** The A6000 and the B300
  already disagree on divergence counts; nothing here generalises across silicon.
- **The mechanism above is the standard explanation for shape-dependent nondeterminism**, and this work
  did not instrument the kernels to confirm which reduction changed. What is measured is that
  divergence follows the shape and vanishes when the shape is pinned.
